Hazard data

What it helps answer
Elevation and slope data can help identify areas with steep terrain where landslides may threaten communities or evacuation routes.
Hypothesis
H1 — Hypothesis 1: Landslide Exposure This supports H1 because communities near or below steep slopes may have a higher risk of blocked roads or damaged homes.
Benefit
It helps us identify areas where the terrain may increase landslide risks.
Limitation
The resolution may not show every small slope or local terrain feature.
What it helps answer
River systems and flood zones can help identify communities and transportation routes that may be affected by flooding.
Hypothesis
H2 — Hypothesis 2: Flood Exposure This supports H2 because low-lying communities near rivers may have a higher risk of flooding.
Benefit
We can compare rivers and flood zones with settlements, roads, and bridges.
Limitation
A flood zone does not confirm that flooding is happening at the current time.
What it helps answer
Known landslide locations can be compared with slope, roads, settlements, and satellite imagery. This can help show whether previous landslides have affected evacuation routes.
Benefit
It provides evidence of where landslides have already occurred.
Limitation
Areas without reported landslides may not actually be safe. There may simply be limited reporting or missing data.
What it helps answer
Before-and-after satellite imagery can help identify changes in water coverage, bare ground, buildings, roads, and other infrastructure.
Benefit
Imagery can cover a large area and may show damage that has not been reported.
Limitation
Clouds, shadows, resolution, and the time between images can make the changes difficult to confirm.
What it helps answer
Rainfall records can help show where recent or unusually heavy rainfall may increase the risk of flooding and landslides.
Hypothesis
H1 — Hypothesis 1: Landslide Exposure H2 — Hypothesis 2: Flood Exposure This information can support both H1 and H2 because rainfall may contribute to slope failure and rising water levels.
Benefit
It can help connect a hazard to a specific time period and show whether conditions were becoming worse.
Limitation
Rainfall may vary across short distances, and available weather stations may not cover every community equally.
